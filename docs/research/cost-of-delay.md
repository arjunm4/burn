# Research: cost of delay

Date: 2026-11-10. One web-research pass on cost of delay, expedite economics, dependency scheduling, progress measures and eliciting a value of time.

**Evidence quality:** mixed. The core cost-of-delay ideas come from Reinertsen and Black Swan Farming, but much of what's available is interviews, blogs and secondary summaries; the book itself was not read. Scheduling (crashing, merge bias, earned schedule) is textbook and government-guide material. Nothing found is specific to robotics startups. Labels: **evidence** (primary or first-hand, including a named author's own case or a standard reference), **vendor** (a company describing its product or selling a fix), **inference** (ours). **Unverified** means the claim is reported second-hand or the page could not be opened.

Related: [`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §2.6, §2.10, §7, [`startup-purchasing.md`](startup-purchasing.md).

## 1. Fundamentals

- **Definition (evidence, secondary):** cost of delay (CoD) is a rate in $/time: the change in lifecycle value from finishing one unit of time later. The total loss from a slip is that rate summed over the slip. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_of_delay) · [Lateral Lens](https://thelaterallens.substack.com/p/what-is-cost-of-delay)
- **Reinertsen's decision rule (evidence, interview 2012):** "cycle time is worth $25,000 a week", so spending $500 to expedite a shipment that saves a week is attractive, and so is a $10,000 consultant. Neither payment *is* the cost of delay. Paying less than the CoD for time saved **on the critical path** improves the economics, and the CoD is the most you should pay to hold the schedule. He says gut-feel estimates of that maximum are a poor substitute. [Softhouse interview](https://www.softhouse.se/en/cost-of-delay-don-reinertsen/)
- **"If you only quantify one thing, quantify the cost of delay"** (Reinertsen, *Principles of Product Development Flow*, 2009): widely attributed; exact wording and location **unverified**. [Learning Loop](https://learningloop.io/glossary/cost-of-delay)
- **Accuracy needed is modest (evidence, secondary):** Reinertsen describes trade-off cost curves as having a long flat bottom, so rough estimates still improve decisions; simple sensitivity models "rarely take more than a few hours". [Lateral Lens, estimating](https://thelaterallens.substack.com/p/estimating-cost-of-delay) · [Playbook](https://playbookteam.com/blog/cost-of-delay-project-modeling)
- **Urgency profiles (evidence, Black Swan Farming):** (1) short life cycle, peak reduced by delay (consumer electronics, fashion); (2) long life cycle, peak reduced (first-mover markets); (3) long life cycle, peak unaffected: CoD is roughly constant per week (process automation), the easiest to calculate; (4) **external deadline**: CoD is $0 until the latest safe start, then accrues (their example: $0/week before 1 Sept, ~$7,700/week after, for a 1 Dec deadline and 13-week lead time). Single-day events (Red Nose Day) and seasonal sales that "drop off a cliff" are cliff-shaped. [Urgency profiles](https://blackswanfarming.com/urgency-profiles/)
- **Kanban classes of service (evidence, secondary):** Anderson's four shapes: *expedite* (very high, immediate), *fixed date* (CoD lands on a date; schedule backwards from it), *standard* (roughly linear), *intangible* (flat "until it suddenly isn't"). [Scrum.org](https://www.scrum.org/resources/blog/classes-service-kanban-what-are-they) · [Kanban Tool](https://kanbantool.com/kanban-guide/classes-of-service)
- **Value and urgency multiply, not add (evidence, Arnold):** a zero in either should zero CoD. [Black Swan Farming](https://blackswanfarming.com/cost-of-delay)
- **Maersk Line case (evidence, authors' own report; not independently verified):** $100m IT portfolio. CoD per requirement followed a power law: the top 25% were worth ~1,000× the bottom 25%. Examples: $210,000/week for one feature (82 hours of work, 46 weeks end to end, 38 of them waiting); $78,846/week and $7,692/week for two others. Reported results: cycle time 208 → ~104–108 days; one team's lead time 168 → 60 days. The paper's own numbers vary between sections. Triage targeted under a week, on one side of A5. [Arnold & Yüce paper](https://blackswanfarming.com/black-swan-farming-using-cost-of-delay) · [experience report](https://blackswanfarming.com/experience-report-maersk-line/)
- **CD3 / WSJF:** CD3 = CoD ÷ duration, used to *sequence* work through a shared constraint. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_of_delay). SAFe's WSJF replaces CoD with (business value + time criticality + risk reduction) on a Fibonacci scale. [SAFe (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20161225132214mp_/http://www.scaledagileframework.com/wsjf)
- **Criticisms of WSJF:** Arnold: adding value and time criticality "doesn't make economic sense"; Fibonacci has no basis for value; "time criticality" is undefined for fixed deadlines, which should be near zero until shortly before the start point and then rise sharply. [Black Swan Farming on SAFe](https://blackswanfarming.com/safe-and-weighted-shortest-job-first-wsjf/). A practitioner shows that estimate errors are amplified by the ratio, flipping rankings (inference by the author; illustrative). [Fail Fast Move On](https://failfastmoveon.blogspot.com/2021/03/why-wsjf-is-nonsense.html). Shalloway (PMI Disciplined Agile): an item with no customer value has no CoD; WSJF on whole epics ignores partial release. [PMI](https://www.pmi.org/disciplined-agile/improving-wsjf)

**Shapes found:** constant rate (linear loss), rising or falling rate (peak eroded), zero then step at a deadline, single-day window, flat-then-sudden. No source treats test-slot booking specifically; the fixed-date and single-day shapes are the nearest analogues (inference).

## 2. Estimating a $/day in practice

- **Few know it; estimates disagree widely (evidence, Reinertsen's own observation; no published survey method):** ~85% of product managers don't know their CoD; intuitive estimates within one team "typically differ by 50 to 1". [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_of_delay) citing Lean Magazine 2011–12 · [Softhouse](https://www.softhouse.se/en/cost-of-delay-don-reinertsen/). The 50:1 has no published dataset behind it (**unverified as a measured statistic**).
- **One documented run of the exercise (evidence, practitioner):** at a hardware/software company, finance put a project's lifecycle profit at ~$16M; asked privately for the cost of one month's delay, senior engineers, finance and product managers answered **$0 to $16M**, most $150K–$2M. The extremes came from hidden assumptions ("we lose the design win entirely" vs "ramp is slow, we can manage expectations"). Took 15–30 minutes. No second round, so no evidence the spread narrowed. [Yeret facilitator guide](https://yeret-agility-site.pages.dev/blog/don-reinertsens-cost-of-delay-intuition-exercise-a-facilitators-guide)
- **Who sets it:** Arnold says to involve finance for peak benefits and to share the assumptions; the point is "shared" rather than "hidden" assumptions, not precision. [Four steps](https://blackswanfarming.com/four-steps-to-quantifying-cost-of-delay). Reinertsen's "use decision rules to decentralize economic control" (a computed $/time handed to the team) is reported second-hand (**unverified** wording). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_of_delay). No source names a role for small startups.
- **Lost-profit method (evidence, secondary):** "a six-month delay can cost 33% of lifecycle profit" (Reinertsen, McKinsey study 1983) is repeatedly cited but the original was not found (**unverified**). [Air University journal](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/ASPJ/journals/Volume-35_Issue-4/RM-Goljan.pdf) · [Playbook](https://playbookteam.com/cost-of-delay)
- **Hardware calculator (vendor):** Instrumental's calculator sums employee expense (headcount × salary), lost sales during the peak, and one-off costs (tooling, extra units). Its examples: a 30-person "SmallCo" at $110K gives $12,644/day of staff cost and $28K/day of lost sales. This is the salary-summing approach Burn rejects; listed for completeness. [Instrumental](https://instrumental.com/resources/delays/find-out-what-one-day-is-worth-with-the-cost-of-delay-calculator)
- **EVT/DVT/PVT slips (vendor, from 100+ interviews, no counts):** a broken EVT→DVT→PVT sequence caused up to 15 weeks' delay; a critical reliability failure adds 6–9 weeks of build plus 2–4 of tool revision; parts-quality mismatches 4 days–1 week; 1–2 critical problems per program are typical. [Instrumental](https://instrumental.com/blog/2018/2/20/why-hardware-products-get-delayed-evt-dvt-pvt-decoded-part-2)
- **Burn rate / runway:** runway = cash ÷ net burn; milestones must fit inside it with time to raise (start at 12–15 months of runway). [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/08/does-your-startup-have-enough-runway-5-factors-to-consider/amp/) · [dev.to](https://dev.to/sclaydon/how-to-calculate-your-startup-runway-and-what-to-do-when-its-short-22ip). **No source found** that states "a day of delay costs a day of runway" or derives a startup's $/day from burn; that is our inference, and it values only the burn, not a missed raise or market window, which can be far larger.
- **Typical ranges for startups or robotics: none found.** Published figures are enterprise ($25K/week in Reinertsen's example; $7.7K–$210K/week at Maersk) or vendor illustrations.

## 3. Expedite economics

- **Project crashing (evidence, textbook):** crash cost per day = (crash cost − normal cost) ÷ (normal time − crash time). Only critical activities are worth crashing; crash the cheapest per day first, re-check the critical path after each step (a new path can become critical), and stop when crash cost per day exceeds the indirect cost saved per day. [MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/docs/project-crashing-time-cost/) · [ProjectManagement.com](https://www.projectmanagement.com/articles/488449/project-crashing-using-critical-path-method)
- **Linearity is an approximation (evidence, course notes):** CPM assumes each day saved costs the same; real crash curves are often stepped. [same](https://metricgate.com/docs/project-crashing-time-cost/). Vendor tiers are discrete steps (e.g. 10 days vs 5 days), not a curve (inference; see [`startup-purchasing.md`](startup-purchasing.md) §1).
- **Non-bottleneck time is worthless (evidence, secondary):** Goldratt's *The Goal*: an hour saved at a non-bottleneck is "a mirage". Exact wording **unverified**. [Antoine Buteau summary](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/the-goal-book-summary/)
- **Expediting as a symptom (evidence, academic):** in MRP research, expediting is how plants absorb in-lead-time changes ("nervousness"); freezing the schedule or charging for changes dampens it best in simulation. [Li & Disney 2016](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/95954/1/Revisiting%20Rescheduling%20MRP%20Nervousness%20and%20the%20Bullwhip%20Effect%20pre-print.pdf) · [Blackburn et al., Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.32.4.413). A CPIM study guide names "expediting without pegging" (paying premium freight for the wrong order) as a common mistake (practitioner). [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/cpim/mrp-capacity/changes-supply-disruptions)
- **How much is spent:** APQC benchmarks expedited cost as a % of freight cost; top performers ~3% (snippet only; peer group **unverified**). [APQC](https://www.apqc.org/what-we-do/benchmarking/open-standards-benchmarking/measures/expedited-costs-percentage-freight). "49% of expedites trace to bad forecasts" is a vendor claim with no source (**unverified**). [Fleetrabbit](https://fleetrabbit.com/article/premium-freight-root-cause-analysis)
- **Does an expedite change the finish date? No measured data found.** The closest is a Berkeley study design on early vs late parts in capital projects (no results published on that page). [Jabbari & Kaminsky 2016](https://projectproduction.org/journal/the-cost-and-impact-of-earliness-and-delays-in-materials-and-parts-delivery/). Proxy: schedule-quality rules of thumb say 10–20% (at most ~25%) of activities sit on the critical path and under 20% are near-critical (float < ~10 days) (vendor and forum; not a standard). [SmartPM](https://help.smartpm.com/critical-path-percentage) · [Planning Planet](https://planningplanet.com/comment/8233). If that holds, most items have float and expediting them buys nothing (inference).

## 4. Dependencies and critical chain

- **Critical path and float (evidence, GAO):** GAO's schedule guide treats total float, free float and near-critical thresholds as program-specific (e.g. a 5-day threshold for a short schedule). [GAO critical path](https://gaoinnovations.gov/schedule-guide/the-critical-path-method.html) · [GAO float](https://gaoinnovations.gov/schedule-guide/float-management.html)
- **Merge bias (evidence, GAO and Hulett):** where parallel paths join, the chance of starting on time is the product of each path's chance, so it falls with every extra input (GAO example: two paths each 60% on time give less than either). CPM therefore understates finish dates; one example gives a CPM path of 17 days vs a PERT expectation of 21; one illustration shows a 35% on-time chance with symmetric uncertainty. Drivers: risk per path, free float before the merge, number of merging paths. [GAO merge bias](https://gaoinnovations.gov/schedule-guide/merge-bias-and-schedule-underestimation.html) (page returned 403 to us; content from search snippet) · [Hulett, PMI](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/project-schedule-risk-analysis-simulation-4620) · [Tensix](https://tensix.com/schedule-risk-analysis-what-is-it-and-why-do-it/)
- **Critical chain (evidence, secondary):** Goldratt strips padding from tasks, pools it as a **project buffer** at the end, and puts **feeding buffers** where non-critical chains join the critical chain; progress is tracked as buffer consumed vs chain completed (fever chart). Rationale: student syndrome and Parkinson's law waste per-task padding. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_chain_project_management) · [HotPMO](https://www.hotpmo.com/management-models/the-critical-chain-method/) · [critical-chain-projects.com](https://www.critical-chain-projects.com/medias/fichiers/overview_of_the_concept.pdf)
- **Test as the constraint (evidence, secondary):** Reinertsen: testing is "probably the single most common critical-path queue" in R&D; ~98% of developers don't know their queue sizes; queue time rises steeply with utilisation. [Medium summary](https://medium.com/prodman-notes/creating-lean-product-development-from-fundamentals-300c188ed6f3) · [QCon slides](https://qconsf.com/sf2011/dl/qcon-sanfran-2011/slides/DonReinertsen_UnderstandingTheMagicOfLeanProductDevelopment.pdf)
- **Test-slot cliffs:** no source models discrete test-slot booking directly. Black Swan's fixed-date and single-day profiles and CPM's resource-constrained scheduling are the nearest (inference).

## 5. Progress-based urgency

- **Earned value SPI is flawed late (evidence, Lipke):** cost-based SPI always returns to 1.0 at completion even for late projects (one example finished seven months late with SPI($) = 1.0 vs SPI(t) = 0.72). Earned schedule, SPI(t) = earned schedule ÷ actual time, stays valid. Schedule indicators are often considered unreliable from about two-thirds through. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earned_schedule) · [Lipke slides](https://www.earnedschedule.com/Docs/April%2019_Earned%20Schedule_Walt%20Lipke.pdf) · [PMI](https://projectcontrolsonline.com//images/white_paper/Earned%20Schedule%20-%20PMI.pdf)
- **Line of balance (evidence, textbook):** an objective chart of cumulative units due, a line of balance worked backwards from it, and a progress chart showing each control point against the line, so lagging streams feeding a shared delivery stand out. [Mosaic Projects](https://mosaicprojects.com.au/WhitePapers/WP1021_LOB.pdf) · [SLM](https://slm.mba/mmpc-009/use-line-of-balance-monitoring-batch-production) · [NASA](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19950014331/downloads/19950014331.pdf)
- **Burn-up / buffer fever charts:** CCPM's fever chart compares streams by buffer consumed vs work completed (see §4). No source found that ranks parallel work streams against each other for one milestone; earned value, LOB and fever charts each compare a stream against its *own plan* (inference).
- **Percent-complete is optimistic (evidence, MIT model):** the "90% syndrome": projects reach ~90% on schedule, then stall and take about twice as long; seen in software, construction, consumer electronics and semiconductors; rework (30–50% of software effort) is a cause. [Ford & Sterman](https://www.mit.edu/~jsterman/90_Syndrom_CERA2003_1.pdf) · [Wiegers](https://modernanalyst.com/Resources/Articles/tabid/115/ID/5357/Ninety-Percent-Done.aspx). Agile practice counts only done / not done for this reason. [Mountain Goat](https://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/blog/why-agile-teams-put-so-much-emphasis-on-being-done-each-iteration)
- **Inferring progress from tickets or chat:** no study found that measures progress inference from issue trackers plus chat. Nearest: DevNous, an LLM multi-agent system that grounds project management in conversation [arXiv 2508.08761](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.08761); an LLM labelling developer chats resolved/unresolved at 93% on 400 Discord conversations (not opened; **unverified**) [author page](https://leo-lihao.github.io/files/P1.pdf).

## 6. Eliciting a value of time

- **Make assumptions shared (evidence, Arnold; Yeret):** the value of the exercise is surfacing the hidden assumption behind each number (design-win loss vs slow ramp), not precision. [Four steps](https://blackswanfarming.com/four-steps-to-quantifying-cost-of-delay) · [Yeret](https://yeret-agility-site.pages.dev/blog/don-reinertsens-cost-of-delay-intuition-exercise-a-facilitators-guide)
- **Ranges over points (evidence, practitioner):** ask for low/high (5%/95%) bounds; ranges "good enough to rank" suffice. [Lateral Lens, estimating](https://thelaterallens.substack.com/p/estimating-cost-of-delay)
- **Overconfidence (evidence, JDM study):** people's 90% intervals are too narrow; 70% intervals are roughly calibrated (Budescu & Du 2007, as cited). Asking for bounds before the best guess (as SHELF does, to reduce anchoring) did **not** improve calibration in three experiments (N = 550). [JDM 2020](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/20/200516/jdm200516.html) · [SHELF comparison, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.11365)
- **Switching values (evidence, HM Treasury Green Book):** standard appraisal practice reports the value a key input would need to take for the recommended option to change. 2026 edition has a section "Sensitivity analysis and switching values". [Green Book 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ae0ba897b7111c482d80a3b/The_Green_Book_2026.pdf) · [definition](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Switching_value)
- **Decisions without agreed probabilities (evidence, RAND):** Robust Decision Making runs the analysis backwards: start from a candidate action, find the conditions under which it fails, and show those to decision makers instead of asking them to agree on probabilities. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robust_decision-making) · [Lempert slides](https://archives.waterinstitute.ufl.edu/symposium2014/downloads/Presentations/Lempert_%20WI_2014.pdf) · [RAND brief](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_briefs/RB9701.html) (403 to us)

## What this means for Burn's design as decided

Mapping only; no changes proposed.

| Step (architecture §2.6, §2.10, §7) | Findings that support or inform it |
|---|---|
| (1) Admissibility | Crash steps are discrete and bounded (§3); vendor tiers are the steps. |
| (2) Dominance: ΔT_m = 0 → cheapest | Crashing only pays on the critical path; Goldratt's non-bottleneck "mirage"; Reinertsen's rule applies to time saved on the critical path (§1, §3). Rules of thumb put most activities off the critical path (§3, weak). Merge bias (§4) is the mechanism behind case B: the latest input sets the merge. |
| (3) Owner $/day vs break-even | Reinertsen's rule is exactly "pay less than CoD per unit of time saved" (§1). The 50:1 spread and the $0–$16M exercise support not inventing a number and asking the owner (§2). The crash-cost slope is the same quantity as Burn's break-even (§3). Salary-summing appears only in vendor calculators (Instrumental), which Burn already rejects. |
| (4) Owner value curve, cliffs | Urgency profiles and classes of service give the shapes: linear, deadline step, single-day window, flat-then-sudden (§1). Case E's test-slot cliff matches the fixed-date / single-day shapes; testing is the most common critical-path queue (§4). |
| Switching conditions, no probabilities (§2.7) | Green Book switching values and RAND RDM are established practice for the same idea (§6); case D's "Expedited wins iff M ≤ 8" is a switching value. |
| Asking the owner (§8) | Ranges with stated assumptions beat points; intervals run narrow; bounds-first ordering didn't help in one study (§6). |
| Track 1: progress ranking | Line of balance and CCPM fever charts are the nearest precedents, but they compare streams to their own plan, not to each other (§5). The 90% syndrome warns that ticket share and stated progress run optimistic (§5). No evidence found on inferring progress from chat. |
| Track 2: dependency graph | CPM float, merge bias, feeding buffers (§4); re-check the critical path after each change, since a new path can become critical (§3). |

## Numbers: well sourced vs weak

**Well sourced (primary or standard reference):**
- Crash cost per day formula and critical-path-only rule (textbook).
- Merge-point on-time probability falls with each merging path (GAO, Hulett).
- Cost-based SPI ends at 1.0 for late projects; SPI(t) does not (Lipke).
- Black Swan's urgency profiles and the $7,700/week deadline example (author's own).
- Reinertsen's $25,000/week, $500 expedite, "pay less than CoD for critical-path time" (his 2012 interview).
- 90% intervals overprecise; bounds-first no better (JDM 2020, N = 550).

**Weak or unverified:**
- "85% don't know their CoD" and "50:1" (Reinertsen's observation; no survey method published).
- "Six months' delay costs 33% of lifecycle profit" (original McKinsey source not found).
- Maersk: top 25% worth ~1,000× bottom 25%, cycle-time halving (authors' own report; internal inconsistencies).
- $0–$16M spread in one workshop (single practitioner account).
- 10–20% of activities critical, <20% near-critical (vendor and forum rules of thumb).
- APQC top performers ~3% expedited freight (snippet only); "49% of expedites from bad forecasts" (vendor, unsourced).
- Instrumental's $12,644/day and 15-week EVT/DVT slips (vendor; salary-based).
- "A day of delay costs a day of runway" (our inference; no source found).
- LLM chat-resolution accuracy of 93% (paper not opened).
